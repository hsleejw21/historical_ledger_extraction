# v14 — Formal L1–L4 Coding Protocol

> **Purpose (per the professor's task).** Make the mapping between the Oxford archive and the
> Four-Level Framework *transparent, reproducible, and defensible*. For every historical proxy we
> answer four questions, and the fourth is the important one: **what evidence would force us to
> reject the classification?** A construct we cannot imagine falsifying is not a measurement, it is
> a label. v13's `MAPPING.md` gave the reasoning (Q1–Q3); this document adds the falsification
> test (Q4) and resolves the L4 boundary by splitting it into L4A and L4B.

Each entry follows the same template:
- **Q1 — Original AI definition** (from the essay).
- **Q2 — Historical analogue / ledger proxy.**
- **Q3 — Evidence that supports the mapping.**
- **Q4 — Evidence that would falsify it** (and where the case would then belong).

---

## L1 — Automation

**Q1. AI definition.** The same activity, performed more efficiently. Substitution without process
redesign. Value driver: **cost**. The ceiling is the cost of the thing being replaced.

**Q2. Oxford analogue / proxy.** Routine operational upkeep that the college always performed and
kept performing: building maintenance, domestic service, provisioning, and externalised utilities
(the historical equivalents the professor named: gas lighting, waterworks, insurance).
*Proxy:* expenditure share on `maintenance + domestic`, with payment regularity as a secondary
standardisation signal.

**Q3. Supports the mapping if:** the function predates the period and persists (lighting, cleaning,
repair existed in 1700 and still exist in 1900); its **purpose is unchanged**; only the **execution
or unit cost** changes; there is **no new revenue** and **no repositioning of who decides what**.

**Q4. Falsified if:** the same spending is tied to a **redesigned workflow** (e.g. centralised
procurement that moves decision rights) — then it is **L3**; or it **creates a new revenue stream**
(e.g. the college sells surplus gas) — then it is **L4A**; or the upkeep share moves because the
institution's **purpose** shifted rather than its efficiency — then it is **L4B**.
*Worked case (the professor's):* gas lighting is L1 because lighting already existed and only its
delivery improved; it would **not** be L1 if it changed how teaching was delivered, or if it became
something the college charged others for.

---

## L2 — Personalisation / Differentiation

**Q1. AI definition.** Differentiated outputs delivered to individuals, using individual-level
information, within an unchanged process. Value driver: **revenue / better demand alignment**.

**Q2. Oxford analogue / proxy.** Provision that differs *by named individual*: scholarships,
exhibitions, prizes, and individually-assessed allowances. *Proxy:* expenditure share on
individual awards (scholar / exhibition / prize lines and educational support tied to a named
person), supported by `person_name` density.

**Q3. Supports if:** what each individual receives **varies** (one student gets a scholarship,
another does not); the **underlying process is unchanged** (the college still teaches and examines
the same way); the differentiation is recorded **against individuals**, not departments.

**Q4. Falsified if:** the differentiation required **redesigning the workflow** (a new examination
system that reorganised teaching) — then **L3**; or it became a **new market / revenue model**
(charging individuals fees as a business, not awarding them support) — then **L4A**. This is a real
boundary: *scholarships to individuals are L2; composition/examination fees as a revenue stream are
L4A.* We keep them on opposite sides of the income/expenditure line to avoid mixing them.

---

## L3 — Operational Innovation

**Q1. AI definition.** The workflow itself is redesigned: steps are re-sequenced, decision rights
move, and human roles are repositioned from execution to supervision and coordination. Value
driver: **process performance**.

**Q2. Oxford analogue / proxy.** The institution's operating model becoming more structured and
coordinated: a permanent administration, and an account that is itself more reconciled — more
separate managed accounts, more sub-totalling, the adoption of double-entry. *Proxy:* administrative
expenditure share plus an accounting-structure index (distinct `section_header` per page,
subtotal ratio), with coordinating-role salaries.

**Q3. Supports if:** the **structure** of the operation changes (new managed accounts, systematic
reconciliation, audit terms), evidencing a redesigned workflow and roles that coordinate rather
than merely execute.

**Q4. Falsified if:** the rise is only **more of the same** — paying more clerks inside an unchanged
process (that is headcount/cost, closer to L1) — or the added structure is **cosmetic** (more pages,
identical logic). To count as L3 we need evidence of genuine reorganisation (double-entry adoption,
new account categories), not volume alone. If the administrative change exists **to launch a new
revenue business**, the revenue part is L4A.

---

## L4 — Business Innovation (split into L4A and L4B)

The professor's most important conceptual instruction: **do not force L4 into one category.** The
AI definition of L4 is strictly *a new business that did not exist before*. At Oxford two different
things both look like "the top level," and they are not the same, so we measure them separately.

### L4A — Business-Model Innovation (the strict AI definition)

**Q1. AI definition.** A genuinely new business / revenue / value-capture mechanism that did not
exist before. Test: *"if the business existed before, it is not L4."* Value driver: **new markets**.

**Q2. Oxford analogue / proxy.** Income from activities the college did **not** run in 1700:
securities investment (consols from 1745, canal 1809, railway 1845) and education sold for **fees**
(composition, examination, tuition). *Proxy:* (securities income + fee income) / total income, with
a **first-appearance** check to enforce the newness test.

**Q3. Supports if:** the revenue logic is **absent early and present late**, and is a **new way of
capturing value** (the college becomes an investor; education becomes a paid service), not an old
income grown larger.

**Q4. Falsified if:** the "new" income is **relabelled old income** (rents or interest recategorised),
or it is a mission activity funded **internally** with **no external market** (then it is L4B, not
L4A). Securities investing is the cleanest L4A because being an investor is a new business; fee
income is L4A only where it is genuinely a market transaction.

### L4B — Mission Transformation (an Oxford dimension, not a literal AI level)

**Q1. Definition (note).** There is **no direct AI counterpart**. L4B is the change in the
institution's **purpose** — what it exists to do — rather than a new market. We flag this honestly:
the AI framework's L4 is business-model innovation (L4A); "mission" is a historically specific
dimension we add because Oxford's purpose visibly shifted. Value driver: **relevance / legitimacy**,
not market revenue.

**Q2. Oxford analogue / proxy.** Reallocation toward the educational/teaching purpose: educational
expenditure, scientific instruction, scholarship systems. *Proxy:* educational **expenditure**
share (with scientific-instruction and scholarship signals as support).

**Q3. Supports if:** the institution **devotes more of itself** to education/teaching as a purpose,
visible in expenditure and in the language of the accounts (examinations, scientific chairs).

**Q4. Falsified if:** rising educational spend is just **scaling an old function** with no change in
priority (volume, not purpose), or if it is really a **revenue** play (then L4A). The decisive
empirical question, which we test, is whether L4A and L4B **move together**, **which appears first**,
and **whether one drives the other** — we do not assume they are the same thing.

## Resource slack — an enabler, not a level

Following the professor's inclination, **resource slack is not a transformation level.** Land-rent
income, a diversified revenue base, and financial capacity do not describe a *role intelligence
plays in the operating model*; they describe a **precondition** that makes higher-order change
affordable. Forcing slack into L4 would confuse "what changed" with "what made change possible."
We therefore treat slack as an **enabling mechanism outside the L1–L4 framework**, and report it as
a condition that may gate L3/L4, not as a level.

## Worked category codings (the protocol applied item by item)

The professor's example codes concrete historical categories, not just abstract levels. Below we
run several real ledger items through the protocol. Each row gives the assigned level, the short
reason it qualifies (Q3), and the specific evidence that would move it elsewhere (Q4).

| Historical item | Level | Why it qualifies (Q3) | What would re-classify it (Q4) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gas lighting** | L1 | Lighting already existed; purpose unchanged; only the means improved | If it reorganised teaching hours/space (L3), or was sold on (L4A) |
| **Waterworks / piped water** | L1 | Same need (water), supplied more efficiently; no model change | If it enabled a new service the college charged for (L4A) |
| **Property insurance** | L1 | An existing risk handled more efficiently; routine cost | If insurance income/underwriting became a revenue activity (L4A) |
| **Externalised services** (contracted-out trades) | L1 | Same task, cheaper provider; process unchanged | If outsourcing redesigned the workflow and roles (L3) |
| **Scholarships / exhibitions / prizes** | L2 | Provision differs by named individual; process unchanged | If they reorganised the teaching workflow (L3), or were a fee market (L4A) |
| **Permanent administration + double-entry** | L3 | The operating model itself is restructured and reconciled | If it is only more clerks doing the same thing (L1, headcount) |
| **Examination system** | L3 (with L4A edge) | Redesigns how the institution assesses and coordinates | The *fees* it generates are L4A; the *process* redesign is L3 |
| **Consols / canal / railway investment** | L4A | A new business: the college becomes an investor; absent in 1700 | If the "income" is recategorised old interest/rent (then not new) |
| **Composition / tuition fees** | L4A | Education sold as a paid service; a new value-capture model | If purely internal support with no market, it leans L4B |
| **Educational expansion / scientific chairs / scholarship systems** | L4B | The institution's purpose shifts toward teaching and science | If it is really a revenue play (L4A), or just scaling an old function |
| **Land-rent income / financial slack** | *enabler, not a level* | A precondition that funds higher-order change, not a role in the model | n/a (it is outside L1-L4 by construction) |

The single hardest case is the examination system, which sits on the L3/L4A boundary: its
*process redesign* is L3, while the *fee revenue* it creates is L4A. We split it along that line
rather than forcing it into one cell, which is the same discipline the L4A/L4B split applies.

## How this protocol is used
- Every series in `analysis_v14.py` is built from one of the proxies defined above.
- The **falsification (Q4)** clauses are the boundary rules the L4A/L4B split and the sensitivity
  analysis operationalise: if a measure can be moved across a boundary by a reasonable alternative
  definition, the sensitivity test will show whether our conclusions depend on that choice.
