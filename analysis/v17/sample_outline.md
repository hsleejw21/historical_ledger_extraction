Tentative Structure
1. Introduction
●	AI transformation as the contemporary motivation.
●	The practical challenge of allocating transformation investments.
●	Why historical evidence may provide unique insight.
●	Research question and contributions.
 
2. Conceptual Background
Introduce the Four-Level AI Transformation Framework:
●	L1 Automation
●	L2 Personalization
●	L3 Operational Innovation
●	L4 Business Model Innovation
Rather than presenting these as rigid stages, discuss them as different forms of organizational transformation and different investment opportunities.
 
3. Historical Setting
Introduce:
●	Exeter College,
●	the Industrial Revolution,
●	why this archive provides a unique opportunity to study long-run organizational transformation.
 
4. Data
Present:
●	the ledger archive,
●	LLM extraction,
●	validation,
●	construction of revenue and expenditure portfolios,
●	measurement of L1-L4.
 
5. Empirical Analyses
Organize the empirical findings around one logical progression rather than chronological order.
Possible flow:
1.	Mapping the archive into L1-L4.
2.	Evolution of revenue portfolios.
3.	Evolution of expenditure portfolios.
4.	Dynamic interaction between resource acquisition and resource deployment.
5.	Structural changes in the resource-allocation mechanism.
6.	Robustness analyses.
The emphasis should always be:
What does each analysis teach us about organizational transformation decisions?
 
6. Interpretation
Rather than simply describing Oxford’s history, discuss:
●	how organizations changed their transformation investment priorities,
●	why higher-order transformation became increasingly important,
●	how decision mechanisms appear to change before visible investment portfolios.
 
7. Implications for AI Transformation
This section should become one of the main contributions of the paper.
Discuss:
●	why organizations may become trapped in L1/L2,
●	why operational innovation and business model innovation become increasingly important during major technological revolutions,
●	what historical lessons contemporary organizations may draw.
 
8. Discussion
Discuss:
●	theoretical implications,
●	managerial implications,
●	limitations,
●	future research.
